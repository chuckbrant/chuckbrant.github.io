<p>We travel through rolling Tuscan countryside - olive groves and fig trees - to Lucca, whose massive walls still completely encircle the old city. Inside, the streets still follow the original Roman grid, leading to the Church of San Michele, built directly on the site of the ancient Roman Forum. From there we continue on to Montecatini.</p>

<p>Lucca has actually had three walls over its history. The first was Roman, standing until around 1000 AD; a medieval wall replaced it as the city grew; and the third and final version, built between 1545 and 1650 to hold off Pisa and the Medici of Florence, is the one still standing today - and remarkably, it's the only complete, intact Renaissance-era city wall left in all of Italy. In 1817 the ramparts were planted with a double row of plane trees, and that tree-lined promenade along the top of the walls is now one of the most pleasant walks in Tuscany.</p>
