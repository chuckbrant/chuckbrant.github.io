<p>Verona's old center is a genuine UNESCO World Heritage Site, not just a stop on the way to somewhere else - it earned that listing in 2000 for something fairly rare: an urban plan that's stayed legible for 2,000 years straight, with intact Roman, medieval, Renaissance, and Venetian layers all still visible in the same streets rather than paved over by any one era.</p>

<p>The city started life as a Roman colony around 89 BC, and its enormous first-century amphitheater - the Arena di Verona - is still in near-original condition and still hosting opera under the stars most summers, which is a fairly remarkable thing for a 2,000-year-old building to be doing. </p>

<p></p>

<p>The city's other claim to fame is entirely fictional, but with an odd grain of truth buried in it. Shakespeare set Romeo and Juliet here, but the feuding families themselves may not be pure invention: Dante mentions two real, historically feuding Veronese families, the Cappelletti and the Montecchi, in Purgatorio, written in the early 1300s.</p>
